The Tooth Monger

All Edward's life he'd been told stories about the beloved visitor that would come to collect his fallen baby teeth.

The Tooth Monger.

Whenever a child lost a tooth, they were to place it beneath their pillow before going to sleep. By morning, the tooth would be gone, replaced with a shining copper coin. The adults spoke of the Tooth Monger with warm smiles and gentle voices, as if it were a dear old friend that visited every family.

"Mind your manners," his mother would say. "The Tooth Monger likes polite children."

"And never try to stay awake to see it," his father would add. "The Tooth Monger does not like being watched."

Edward had always thought this was nonsense.

When he lost his first tooth at seven years old, he carefully wrapped it in a scrap of cloth and tucked it beneath his pillow. That night he slept fitfully, too excited to rest.

When dawn arrived, he thrust his hand beneath the pillow.

The tooth was gone.

In its place sat a dull copper coin.

Edward stared at it for a long time.

Perhaps his parents really had put it there.

But if they had, how had they removed the tooth without waking him?

And what if the Tooth Monger was real?

He imagined a beautiful creature no larger than a bird. Perhaps it had shimmering wings and silver hair. Maybe it floated through keyholes and danced across moonbeams.

The thought filled him with wonder.

Over the next year, Edward lost four more teeth.

Each time the tooth vanished.

Each time a copper coin appeared.

And each time his parents reminded him:

"Never stay awake for the Tooth Monger."

The warning only made him more curious.

By the time his sixth tooth became loose, Edward had devised a plan.

He would fool the Tooth Monger.

That afternoon he searched the garden until he found a pebble the same size and shape as his tooth. He painted it white and polished it carefully until it gleamed.

That night, after his parents had kissed him goodnight, he slipped the stone beneath his pillow.

Then he waited.

Hours passed.

The house settled with creaks and groans.

The wind rattled the shutters.

Edward fought against sleep until his eyes burned.

Midnight came and went.

Nothing happened.

He was beginning to think the Tooth Monger wasn't real after all when he heard it.

A wet scraping sound.

Slow.

Deliberate.

Dragging itself across the floor below.

Scrrrrk.

Scrrrrk.

Scrrrrk.

Edward sat upright in bed.

The sound moved through the house.

Past the front door.

Across the hallway.

Up the stairs.

Each scrape was accompanied by a soft clicking noise, like thousands of tiny pebbles knocking together.

Click-click-click-click.

The sound stopped outside his bedroom door.

Edward held his breath.

The handle slowly turned.

The door creaked open.

Moonlight spilled into the room.

Something entered.

Edward's imagination shattered.

The Tooth Monger was no fairy.

It was a heap.

A crawling mound of teeth.

Human teeth.

Thousands of them.

Yellow teeth.

Black teeth.

Crooked teeth.

Children's teeth.

They protruded from folds of swollen pink gums woven together into a grotesque body.

The creature possessed no eyes.

No face.

No legs.

Only a single gangly arm extended from its center. The limb was impossibly long and slick with saliva. Its fingers bent in too many places and ended in jagged umkept fingernails.

The thing dragged itself forward using that arm.

Scrrrrk.

Click-click-click.

Its body shifted as countless teeth ground against one another.

Edward quickly squeezed his eyes shut.

He tried his best to feign sleep, but he was certain that his deafening heartbeat would give him away.

The creature stopped beside his bed.

The long arm reached beneath his pillow.

It withdrew the painted stone.

For a moment, everything became still.

Edward gathered the courage to peek at the creature and watched as the the arm slowly clenched, crushing the pebble into dust.

A low grinding noise emerged from somewhere within the mound.

The arm rose.

And pointed directly at Edward.

The boy's blood turned to ice.

The creature knew.

The arm shot forward.

Its fingers clamped over Edward's mouth, pinning him to the bed.

He finally managed to scream, but the sound was swallowed by the creature's grip.

The fingers pried his jaw open.

Edward thrashed, kicked, and cried.

The Tooth Monger did not care.

One clawed finger hooked around his loosest tooth.

Then pulled.

A sharp pain exploded through his mouth.

The tooth came free with a wet pop.

The creature deposited it into its body, where it vanished among the countless others.

Edward sobbed.

Another finger entered.

Another tooth came free.

Then another.

And another.

One by one.

Each tooth torn from its socket.

The Tooth Monger worked patiently.

Methodically.

As though harvesting fruit from a tree.

The pile of teeth within its body clicked happily with every addition.

By the time it finished, every baby tooth Edward still possessed was gone.

Blood dribbled down his chin.

His gums ached.

The creature reached beneath the pillow one final time.

When its arm withdrew, a handful of copper coins spilled onto the blanket.

Payment.

Then it turned.

Scraped across the floor.

And disappeared into the darkness.

Edward remained awake until sunrise.

When his parents entered his room, they found him sitting upright in bed, pale and trembling.

The copper coins lay scattered around him.

His pillow was stained red.

His mother quietly gathered the coins.

His father stared at Edward's empty gums.

Neither looked surprised.

Neither asked what had happened.

His father merely sighed.

Then he said the same words his own father had once told him:

"The Tooth Monger does not like being watched."

From that day forward, Edward never spoke of what he had seen.

Yet years later, whenever he noticed a child proudly showing off a loose tooth, he would always offer the same warning.

"Put the tooth beneath your pillow."

"And whatever you do..."

"Don't try to stay awake."
